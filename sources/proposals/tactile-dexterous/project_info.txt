OVERVIEW

Title:
SoTa: Soft Tactile Skins for Dexterous Manipulation

One-sentence project description:
SoTa develops low-cost, custom-fit but layout-aligned full-hand tactile skins for human and robot hands, enabling collection of paired bimanual visuo-tactile demonstrations and cross-embodiment co-training of policies for contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks.

Overleaf link for references:
—

The SoTa draft introduces 202-taxel tactile skins with the same semantic taxel layout across human and robot hands and evaluates their use for co-training on contact-rich tasks including box closing, cloth folding, pepper placement, cup picking, and board wiping. The current draft contains an internal inconsistency in dataset size: the abstract/introduction state approximately 6 hours, while the dataset analysis reports 1,039 episodes and approximately 2.5 hours; this should be reconciled before submission.

COMPUTATIONAL READINESS AND TUNING STATUS

Implementation status: whether the training pipeline is already running and tested.
The SoTa draft specifies a visuo-tactile policy pipeline based on Diffusion Policy, taking as input egocentric RGB observations, proprioception, and 202-taxel tactile readings and producing wrist and finger action targets. The draft reports initial physical-robot evaluation results for cloth folding and pepper placement, indicating that at least part of the training and evaluation pipeline is operational. However, implementation completeness for all five tasks and all scaling studies remains to be confirmed.  

The original computational proposal additionally reports an already implemented distributed training pipeline for DreamZero world/action model training, with preliminary runs executed and profiled. Its direct use for SoTa is — / to be confirmed.  

Profiling evidence: any existing runs showing GPU utilization, VRAM usage, throughput, runtime, or scaling behavior.
For the compute profile inherited from the original proposal, an example DreamZero training run on a GCP a3-highgpu-2g instance achieved approximately 86% GPU utilization, saturated approximately 80 GB H100 VRAM, and showed loss approaching convergence after approximately 3 days. No SoTa-specific profiling results for VRAM usage, throughput, runtime, or scaling behavior are reported in the current draft.

Scaling need: target dataset/model scale and why current resources are insufficient.
For SoTa, the scientific scaling need is to evaluate whether increasing the quantity of aligned human visuo-tactile data improves robot-policy performance under a fixed robot-data budget, across multiple contact-rich dexterous tasks. The data collection approach is intended to make human tactile demonstrations substantially easier to scale than robot teleoperation.

Under the inherited compute proposal, the longer-term scaling target includes training 3–8B parameter dexterous world/action models on large-scale human and robot video datasets. Current lab resources are insufficient because they do not provide adequate H100 availability, suffer from contention, and cannot reliably support multi-day, multi-GPU experimental campaigns with high-bandwidth inter-GPU communication.  

Requested infrastructure benefit: what additional experiments or scale the requested compute will enable.
Requested compute would enable systematic SoTa experiments across the full five-task evaluation suite, including robot-only, human-only, and human-plus-robot co-training regimes; scaling studies over increasing human-data fractions; repeat training runs and physical-robot evaluations; and, subject to final architecture decisions, larger visuo-tactile or world/action model experiments using aligned human and robot tactile demonstrations. Access to H100-class multi-GPU nodes would additionally reduce training turnaround time, support concurrent ablations, and provide stable execution for longer-running training campaigns.
